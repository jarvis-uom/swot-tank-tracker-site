Drone survey

A drone mapped one tank. Satellites can do it for every tank.

In 2026 the Irrigation Department used a drone survey to draw Kanhindawewa's area and capacity curve. Our project makes the same kind of curve from satellites, for every tank SWOT sees, and keeps it up to date.

The survey

Area capacity curve, Kanhindawewa tank

Irrigation Department, Geo Informatics Branch. Huruluwewa Division, Anuradhapura Region. Drawing 491/15/2A, dated 25 June 2026.

  • How it was made: a 3D model of the ground (a DEM) made from LiDAR data collected by a drone, and from GNSS survey data.
  • The map: scale 1:3,000, on the Sri Lanka Grid 1999 (SLD99), with a contour every 1 ft of height.
  • The table: the water area and capacity at every 1 ft, from the sluice sill at 249.5 ft to 261 ft, and the capacity at every 0.1 ft.

The same job from space

Two ways to the same table

Both end with the water area and capacity at each level of the tank. They get there in different ways.

Drone survey

  1. A drone with LiDAR flies over the tank
  2. A 3D model of the ground, from LiDAR and GNSS data
  3. A shoreline at every 1 ft of water level
  4. The area and capacity at each level

Satellites (our project)

  1. SWOT measures the water height on each pass
  2. Radar and photos measure the water area
  3. Each picture day adds one dot: a height and an area
  4. The dots give the area and capacity at each level

A drone survey is the most detailed map of one tank. Satellites give the same kind of table for every tank, and keep it up to date. Surveys like this one are the reference our satellite curves are compared with.
