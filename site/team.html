Member A · water maps

Where the water is

RQ1 How accurately can deep learning map tank water from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2, including tanks under cloud or partly covered by water plants?

In plain words

Member A teaches a computer to find the water in satellite pictures, even under cloud and under floating plants. The examples use made-up round numbers.

  1. By hand

    Label the water

    On small squares cut from satellite pictures of our tanks, draw three things: open water, water under plants, and land. These drawings are the answers the model learns from.

    Made for: our own tanks, including cloudy and plant-covered days

    Example

    A square of tank 7 in March: the photo shows green plants over most of the tank. Using the tank's outline and photos from our field visit, we mark the plant-covered part as "water under plants", not land.

  2. Trained modelRQ1

    Train the water map

    A U-Net first learns what water looks like from pictures that others have already labelled: floods and lakes around the world. Then it learns our tanks from our own labels, especially the plant-covered ones. It looks at radar and photos together.

    Learns from: S1S2-Water and Sen1Floods11, then our labels · Used on: our 13 tanks

    Example

    First, thousands of labelled squares from around the world teach it open water.

    Then, a few hundred squares of our own tanks teach it Sri Lankan tanks and the water hidden under plants.

  3. Output

    Map every tank on every picture day

    After every new picture, the model draws the water on each of our 13 tanks and measures its area. Radar sees through cloud. Photos are sharper, but only on clear days.

    Gives: the water area of each tank on every picture day, to Members B and C

    Example

    A cloudy day in November: the photo shows only cloud, but the radar still sees tank 4. The map says 25 ha of water.

    A tank under plants: say a tank holds water over 60 ha, but plants cover two thirds of it. A simple rule on the photo finds only the open part, about 20 ha. Member A's map must also find the 40 ha under the plants.

  4. Test

    Check it

    Compare the map with our own labels, with Kanhindawewa's drone survey (the true area at that day's water height), and with the simple rules on the same days.

    Our test

    At Kanhindawewa, a plant-covered tank, the simple rules were off by 36–100%. Member A's map must do better than that.

Step by step

  1. Find the picture days Build

    List every Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 photo day over our 13 tanks, and NISAR, whose data began on 17 June 2026 (see Picture days).

  2. Download and cut Build

    Cut each picture to the tank, mask the clouds, and add the land slope.

  3. Label the water Data

    Draw open water and "water under plants" on our tanks, using each tank's full-supply outline and photos from field visits.

  4. Pre-train the U-Net Model

    First it learns water from S1S2-Water, a public set of labelled pictures from around the world.

  5. Fine-tune on our tanks Model

    Then it learns our tanks from our own labels, especially the ones covered by plants.

  6. Test it Test

    IoU and F1 against our labels, the area against Kanhindawewa's drone survey, and the simple water rules on the same days.

  7. Hand over Output

    The water area of every tank on every picture day, with how sure the map is.

Member B · SWOT and storage

How much water there is

RQ2 How accurately does SWOT measure water height on major reservoirs and on village tanks close to its 6.25 ha size limit, compared with gauge readings, and how many "suspect" readings can a learned screen keep, and at what error?

RQ3 Can a model trained on about 100 gauged reservoirs turn a tank's SWOT height and AI water area into % full, for tanks it has never seen, more accurately than one area–storage formula?

In plain words

Member B turns SWOT's water height, and Member A's water area, into how full each tank is. The examples use made-up round numbers; the test results come from our model check (October 2026).

  1. CheckRQ2

    Check SWOT's heights

    At the 5 big reservoirs, compare SWOT's height with the gauge on the same day, to see how many cm it is off.

    Uses: SWOT heights and the daily gauges of the 5 big reservoirs

    Example

    On every pass, SWOT reads about 50 cm above the gauge. That steady gap is only a different zero, so we take it away. What is left, about 10 cm up or down, is SWOT's real error.

  2. Trained modelRQ2

    Trust screen

    Almost all of SWOT's readings at the village tanks are marked "suspect". This small model looks at each reading and decides whether to keep it or drop it.

    Learns from: 84 reservoirs with records · Used on: our 13 tanks

    Example

    Tank 4 gets 10 readings this season, all "suspect". The screen keeps the 8 that look right and drops the 2 that are metres off.

    Our test: it kept 85% of the readings with an average error of 15 cm, against 21 cm for SWOT's own flags.

  3. Trained model: the main oneRQ3

    Storage model

    It learns from big and medium reservoirs that have daily records. Then it is used on the small village tanks that have none. That is RQ3.

    Learns from: about 100 reservoirs with records · Used on: the 8 village tanks (and the 5 big ones, as a check)

    Example

    Training, on reservoirs with daily records:

    • Reservoir P, 1 March: water 3 m above its lowest level, outline 500 ha. The department's sheet says 70% full.
    • Reservoir Q, 1 March: 1 m above its lowest level, outline 100 ha. The sheet says 30% full.
    • About 3,000 rows like these, from about 100 reservoirs. The model learns the pattern.

    Using it, on a village tank with no records:

    • Village tank 4, 1 March: 2 m above its lowest level, outline 30 ha. The model says about 50% full.

    Our test: on reservoirs it had never seen, it was off by about 8% of capacity (about 11% on the small ones).

  4. No model: arithmetic

    Slices

    Not a model, just arithmetic, done for each of our 13 tanks with its own data. Each picture day gives one dot: the height (from Member B) and the water area (from Member A).

    Slices give the water gained or lost in m³. They can't give % full, because we don't know the tank's bottom or its total capacity. The storage model gives % full, and the two check each other. More on the How volume works page.

    Example

    Dots at 1 m (20 ha), 2 m (40 ha) and 3 m (60 ha):

    • from 1 to 2 m: middle area 30 ha × 1 m = 300,000 m³;
    • from 2 to 3 m: middle area 50 ha × 1 m = 500,000 m³.

    So if the water rose from 1 m to 3 m, the tank gained 800,000 m³.

Step by step

  1. Collect SWOT heights Build

    Every pass since July 2023, for our 13 tanks and about 100 Irrigation Department reservoirs, from NASA's Hydrocron service (see SWOT data).

  2. Check SWOT against gauges Data

    SWOT minus the gauge on every pass at the 5 big reservoirs: the steady gap between them, and the spread in cm.

  3. Trust screen Model

    A small model scores each reading, so "suspect" readings that are fine are kept and the bad ones are dropped.

  4. Build the training pairs Data

    Each reservoir's daily storage, matched with its SWOT height and its water area on the same day.

  5. Train the storage model Model

    Gradient boosting in which more height or more area never means less water. It only uses inputs every tank has.

  6. Test it Test

    On reservoirs it never saw: the error in % of capacity, against one formula for all tanks, with and without the trust screen.

  7. Hand over Output

    How full every tank is, after every SWOT pass.

Member C · forecast and system

What comes next

RQ4 Can a deep-learning model forecast each tank's storage 2–4 weeks ahead, from recent storage, rainfall and evaporation, better than a "no change" forecast?

In plain words

Member C turns each tank's % full into a forecast for the coming weeks, and runs the whole chain and the dashboard. The examples use made-up round numbers; the test results come from our model check (October 2026).

  1. Build

    Storage line

    After every new picture or SWOT pass, run Member B's model. This gives a % full line over time for each of the 13 tanks.

    Gets: the water area from Member A and the height from SWOT, through Member B's model

    Example

    Tank 4: 60% on 1 February, 55% on 13 February, 50% on 1 March. One dot after every pass, joined into a line.

  2. Trained modelRQ4

    Forecast

    Predict % full 1, 2 and 4 weeks ahead. The inputs are today's % full, the change over the last few weeks, what usually happens at this time of year, and recent rain.

    Member C trains two kinds of model: a deep-learning model (an LSTM) and gradient boosting. Both must beat "no change", which assumes the tank stays where it is.

    Learns from: 117 reservoirs' daily records, 2020–2026 (about 200,000 days) · Used on: our 13 tanks

    Example

    On 1 March a tank is 50% full. It fell 4% in the last 2 weeks, and in past years it usually falls about 4% in the next 2 weeks of March. Little rain has fallen.

    Forecast for 15 March: about 46%. "No change" would say 50%.

    In November, after 100 mm of rain in a week, the same model would predict a rise.

    Our test: gradient boosting was about 10–20% better than "no change", and the LSTM was close behind.

  3. Output

    Dashboard

    A map of the 13 tanks showing today's % full, the trend and the forecast. It updates by itself after every pass.

    Example

    Click tank 4: 50% full today, falling, about 46% in 2 weeks.

It isn't just rain and gauges. In our test, the season and the recent change did most of the work. Past rain helped only a little (1–2%), and knowing the coming rain would help more (6–9%). The model learns that mix by itself; nobody writes a formula.

Step by step

  1. Collect the history Build

    Daily storage for 117 Irrigation Department reservoirs since 2020, with CHIRPS rain and ERA5 evaporation.

  2. Seasonal average Data

    For each tank, its usual storage on each day of the year, built from past years only.

  3. Train the forecast Model

    An LSTM, and a gradient-boosting model to compare it with, for 1, 2 and 4 weeks ahead.

  4. Test it Test

    On years and reservoirs it never saw, against "no change" and the seasonal average.

  5. Run the chain Build

    After every pass, Member A's water map and SWOT's height go into Member B's model, and out comes each study tank's % full.

  6. Build the dashboard Build

    Storage, safe limits and the forecast for every tank, updated by itself with GitHub Actions.

  7. Test the whole chain Test

    From picture to forecast, on the village tanks and on Kanhindawewa's drone survey.
