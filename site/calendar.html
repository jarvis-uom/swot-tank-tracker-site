Satellite calendar

Which days we get data

Two calendars. Satellite days: every day each satellite gave data over our tanks since NISAR started on 17 June 2026, and the days we expect until the end of March 2027. Picture + gauge days: every day since October 2020 when a satellite picture of a big reservoir and its gauge reading fall on the same day. All our tanks share the same passes. Dates are Sri Lanka dates.

How to read it

Tags are pictures; the stripe and the G are heights

    • Tags are water pictures, which give the water's area: radar (S1, NI, AL) and photos (S2, LS). A solid tag is a real day from the satellite's own catalogue; a dashed tag is an expected day from its fixed repeat cycle; a faded photo tag was too cloudy to use.
    • Heights are marked differently: a dark stripe along the top of a day is a SWOT pass, and the small G is the gauge. Pictures plus a height make the dots, and the dots make the layers.
    • Big reservoirs: the Irrigation Department's gauge gives a height every day (the small G in each day). So every satellite day there becomes a dot.
    • Village tanks: SWOT days give the height. Radar and photo days in between take their height from the straight line between two SWOT passes.

    How many days each satellite gives us

    Since NISAR started, at least one satellite passes on about half of all days. Photos only count when the sky is clear, and from November to February that is rare.

    The calendar

    June 2026 to March 2027, day by day

    • Repeat cycles used: SWOT, two passes every 21 days; Sentinel-1, every 12 days; NISAR, every 12 days on each of its two tracks; Sentinel-2B and 2C, every 10 days each, and 2A every 20 days; Landsat 8 and 9, every 16 days each.
    • Plans can change: a satellite can miss a day (NISAR missed one in August 2026), Sentinel-2A's extra passes may stop, and ALOS-2 is not shown ahead because it has no fixed plan we can repeat.

    Since October 2020

    Days with a picture and a gauge reading

    On these days a satellite picture gives the water area, and the Irrigation Department's gauge gives the water stored, on the same day. Each one is a ready-made example to train and test our models. From July 2023, SWOT adds the height on some of them.

      • The dataset: picture-gauge-days.csv (about 180 KB) has one row per picture day for all five reservoirs: the satellite, the photo's cloud cover, the gauge's water stored and its % of capacity that day, and whether SWOT also measured.
      • Cloud cover is for the whole photo, about 100 km across, so a tank can be clear in a cloudy photo, or the other way round. Clear means under 20% cloud; partly cloudy, 20–50%. Radar sees through clouds.
