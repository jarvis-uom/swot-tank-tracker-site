Picture days

How many layers the pictures give

Only the water pictures here: radar on every pass, photos only when the sky is clear. No gauge and no SWOT. Each picture day can become one dot, and each dot adds one layer. Real days since NISAR started on 17 June 2026, expected days to the end of March 2027, over Mahakanadarawa.

Month by month

Picture days from each satellite

Radar sees through cloud, so it gives pictures in every month. Photos count only when the sky over the tank is clear, which is rare from November to February.

Adding up

The dots add up over the months

Each picture day is one more dot on the tank's curve. A day with two pictures counts once.

A rough number of layers

How thick the layers get

  • Each picture still needs a height to become a dot: the gauge every day at the five big reservoirs, or the straight line between two SWOT passes at other tanks.
  • Layers are not even: in the rains the water rises fast, so pictures land far apart in height and layers get thicker. On the slow fall in the dry season they get thinner.
  • Rough on purpose: expected days can change, a satellite can miss a day, and ALOS-2 is not counted ahead because it has no fixed plan.
