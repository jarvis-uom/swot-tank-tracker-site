Water height

How high the water stands. Member B turns it into how full each tank is.

SWOT lake productNASA and CNES · version D · July 2023 onwards

✓ Checked: works for us

What it gives
A water height (and area) for every lake on each pass. SWOT sees all 13 study tanks about twice every 21 days.
Size
One pass file for every lake: about 12 MB. Through Hydrocron a lake's whole record is tiny: all 677 lakes we checked came to 9 MB.
Is it right for us?
  • All 13 study tanks have a SWOT lake ID, with 47–90 usable readings each since July 2023.
  • At the 5 big reservoirs SWOT reads 40–64 cm above the gauges: a steady gap, with only 7–13 cm of scatter.
  • Village tanks get almost only "suspect" readings, so Member B's trust screen cleans them first.
  • Official accuracy: 10 cm is required on lakes over 1 km²; 25 cm is the goal for smaller lakes.
How others used it
  • Das and Hossain, 2025: 245 reservoirs in 5 countries; SWOT height R² 0.91 (usual miss 92 cm)
  • Ma et al., 2026: 12 reservoirs in the western USA; height within 20 cm, storage within 10%
  • Li et al., 2026: 14 reservoirs; storage error 15% of capacity, 3% once tied to each reservoir's records
Ready-made tools
  • SWOT Lake and River Explorer (click any tank)
  • Hydrocron
  • Our SWOT data page

SWOT pixel cloudNASA and CNES · version D

… Stretch: not tested yet

What it gives
A height for every water pixel, including the shoreline. It also covers tanks that have no SWOT lake ID.
Size
About 460–600 MB per file. About 100 files cover each of our tanks since July 2023.
Is it right for us?
  • Only 58% of mapped tanks of 6.25 ha or more have a lake ID; the pixel cloud is how to add the rest.
  • Big files and an Earthdata login, so it stays a stretch goal.
How others used it
  • Köhn and Nielsen, 2026: 37 lakes in Denmark (25 ha to 40 km²); pixel-cloud water levels within 5–6 cm

ICESat-2 laser heightsNASA · ATL13 inland water · 2018 onwards

◦ For checks

What it gives
Water heights along narrow laser tracks. Each track repeats only every 91 days.
Size
About 90–100 MB per file for a whole track. Through SlideRule we took only the points over our tanks: under 50 MB in all.
Is it right for us?
  • Its beams crossed 12 of our 13 tanks: 12–23 dates at each big reservoir, but 0–5 at the village tanks.
  • Against SWOT, 13 of 14 pairs taken within 4 days agree within 15 cm.
How others used it
  • Madumalsha et al., 2025: 9 Sri Lankan reservoirs; correlation 0.99 with the gauges, but 37 cm too high on average
Ready-made tools
  • SlideRule (no login)

Water area: pictures from space

Where the water is. Member A's water map learns from these pictures.

Sentinel-2 photosCopernicus (ESA) · 10 m · 2015 onwards

! Works, with care: clouds and plants

What it gives
Colour and infrared photos at 10 m. Our tanks get about 89 pictures a year (one tile, 44PMQ).
Size
230–640 MB per scene (a 110 km square). We read only small windows around each tank: our check of over 400 scenes read about 200 MB.
Is it right for us?
  • Only 8–12% of scenes are fully clear over Mahakanadarawa. The best months are March and September–October.
  • At plant-covered Kanhindawewa, a simple water index (NDWI) missed about 65% of the water.
  • At 10 m a 6 ha tank is 600 pixels, against 67 at 30 m, so it suits small tanks.
How others used it
  • IFAD and ESA, 2026: monthly 10 m water maps of over 10,650 water bodies in 6 Sri Lankan districts, 2017 to August 2025
  • Dossou Codjia et al., 2025: 20 reservoirs in Burkina Faso; R² 0.76–0.97 at 14, but plants hid the water at 6
  • Pascal et al., 2024: 504 tanks in Telangana, India; a 17% area error became a 31% volume error
Ready-made tools
  • Copernicus Browser (opens on Mahakanadarawa)
  • Dynamic World water class

Sentinel-1 radarCopernicus (ESA) · C-band · 2014 onwards

! Works, with care: plants

What it gives
Radar pictures that see through cloud, day and night. Our tanks get one radar date every 12 days: tracks 19 and 27 pass on the same day, morning and evening. Since June 2026 Sentinel-1D takes these pictures; the new 6-day repeat has not reached our area.
Size
About 1–2 GB per scene. Ready-made terrain-corrected pieces (OPERA RTC-S1) are about 14 MB each.
Is it right for us?
  • It sees the water through monsoon cloud, when photos see nothing.
  • Thick floating plants look like land to it: at Kanhindawewa a simple −18 dB rule found only about a tenth of the water.
How others used it
  • Perera and Jayaweera, 2020: Sri Lankan reservoirs; radar water area against reported volume, R² 0.67–0.94
  • Wieland et al., 2024: a U-Net on radar matched 85% of the true water
Ready-made tools
  • Planetary Computer: terrain-corrected Sentinel-1

L-band radar: NISAR and ALOS-2NASA–ISRO (NISAR, 2025 onwards) · JAXA (ALOS-2, 2014 onwards)

… New: not tested yet

What it gives
Longer-wave radar that sees further into plants. NISAR gives about 5 pictures a month here; free ALOS-2 pictures come about 10 times a year.
Size
NISAR: about 4–5 GB per scene for the map-ready version (GCOV); the raw versions are 15–26 GB. ALOS-2: size not checked.
Is it right for us?
  • NISAR already has pictures over our tanks (October 2026). Its public release covers passes from 17 June 2026; older ones are being released.
  • It should see water under floating plants better than Sentinel-1. We have not tested it on our tanks yet.
  • The free ALOS-2 copy on AWS covers Africa only, so ours would come from Earth Engine.
How others used it
  • NISAR's data only became public in July 2026, so we found no tank study yet.
Ready-made tools
  • ALOS-2 ScanSAR in Google Earth Engine (25 m)

OPERA water mapsNASA · DSWx · 30 m

◦ For checks

What it gives
Ready-made water maps: one from each photo (DSWx-HLS, validated maps since April 2023) and one from each radar picture (DSWx-S1, since August 2024).
Size
About 48 MB per photo-based map and 1–10 MB per radar-based map, for one tile.
Is it right for us?
  • Over our tile: 109 photo-based maps and 26 radar dates in 12 months.
  • 30 m pixels are coarse for small tanks. The radar version has an "inundated vegetation" (water under plants) class, but only for water bodies over 3 ha and 200 m wide. A ready-made baseline for Member A's maps.

Global Water Watch archiveGlobal Water Watch · Landsat and Sentinel-2 · saved September 2026

✓ Checked: works for us

What it gives
Water area over time for 71,208 reservoirs worldwide. The archive was saved before the service closes.
Size
One 527 MB file for every reservoir. Hurulu Wewa's record since October 2020 is 147 areas.
Is it right for us?
  • We matched 69 of the department's 117 reservoirs by their outlines (78% overlap on the median), giving 11,484 area–storage pairs to train Member B's model.
  • Cloud gaps are filled in by its method, not measured. It has no village tanks.
How others used it
  • Donchyts et al., 2022: the method behind it, for small and medium reservoirs worldwide
Ready-made tools
  • Global Water Watch website (offline from November 2026)

JRC Global Surface WaterEuropean Commission JRC · Landsat · 1984 onwards

◦ For history and checks

What it gives
Monthly 30 m water maps since 1984, and how often each pixel was wet.
Size
One 10° × 10° tile of "how often wet": 18 MB.
Is it right for us?
  • Its newest month is December 2024, so it gives history, not new dates.
  • Each tank was fully seen in 21–22 of the 36 months of 2022–24. Its "how often wet" map can give each tank's largest outline.
How others used it
  • Pekel et al., 2016 (Nature): the dataset paper

AI training data

Pictures where people have already drawn the water in. Member A's AI learns from them what water looks like.

AI training data: S1S2-Water and Sen1Floods11DLR (S1S2-Water) · Cloud to Street (Sen1Floods11)

! Works, with care: no water under plants

What it gives
Hand-checked water masks for Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 pictures. S1S2-Water covers 65 areas in 29 countries; Sen1Floods11 covers flood events.
Size
S1S2-Water (v1.0.1): 166 GB in 6 parts of 20–33 GB, plus a catalogue file. Sen1Floods11: about 14 GB. We plan Sen1Floods11 plus one part of S1S2-Water.
Is it right for us?
  • U-Nets trained on S1S2-Water matched 97% of the true water on photos and 85% on radar.
  • Neither has a "water under plants" class, so we add our own labels from our tanks.
How others used it
  • Wieland et al., 2024: the S1S2-Water paper
  • Bonafilia et al., 2020: the Sen1Floods11 paper (CVPR workshops)
Ready-made tools
  • Sen1Floods11 on GitHub

Ground truth

Measured on the ground: what we train on and test against.

Irrigation Department daily storageIrrigation Department of Sri Lanka · Reservoir Status · 2020 onwards

! Checked: works, with care

What it gives
Daily storage of 117 reservoirs (74 major since October 2020, 43 medium since April 2023), with capacity, and daily gauge depth for the big ones.
Size
About 200,000 daily values: a 13 MB table.
Is it right for us?
  • The department's own public dashboards read this same sheet.
  • The Reservoir Status page shows only the latest day, but four tabs of the same sheet keep every day: since October 2020 for the major reservoirs, and since 2023 for the medium ones. The download links are below.
  • Our logger saves every tab of it three times a day, and the department's report as PDF once a week, so nothing is lost if the sheet changes.
  • The value changes on only 34–62% of days at the big reservoirs, so some days are not updated.
  • Its "long-term average" column is the average of the seasons in the sheet, including the latest years. It must not be used to test forecasts.
  • On 27 October 2026 it differed from the Mahaweli Authority's figure for Nachchaduwa by 4%.
How others used it
  • It trains Member B's storage model and Member C's forecast, and labels the trust screen.
Ready-made tools

Irrigation Department tank map (reservoir layer)Irrigation Department of Sri Lanka · "as at 1 January 2022"

! Checked: works, with care

What it gives
A map layer of 354 irrigation schemes: full-supply outline and area, capacity, dead storage, catchment, flood level and bund top.
Size
354 outlines: a 6 MB file.
Is it right for us?
  • 258 tanks have both capacity and area (81 major, 164 medium, 13 minor). 116 of the 117 daily-sheet reservoirs sit on an outline.
  • It gives no survey year. Mahakanadarawa's spill was raised in 2024–26, so its capacity rose from 45 to 47 million m³, but the layer still shows the old level.
  • None of our 8 village tanks is in it (checked 30 October 2026), so their outlines come from OpenStreetMap.

◦ For checks

What it gives
Water level in metres above sea level, and storage, for Hurulu Wewa, Nachchaduwa and Nuwara Wewa.
Size

Ground and tank-bed shape

The land around each tank, and the shape of the bed.

Elevation map: Copernicus DEM (GLO-30)Copernicus · 30 m · radar from 2011–12 here

! Works, with care: no tank beds

What it gives
Ground height every 30 m. Member B takes land slope and catchment from it; Member A uses the slope.
Size
One 1° × 1° tile is 47 MB, and one tile covers all 13 of our tanks.
Is it right for us?
  • Water is flattened: 70–95% of each big reservoir is one flat value, so the bed is missing.
  • On dry beds it is within about 1 m of ICESat-2: the best of the free elevation maps here.
How others used it
  • Pascal et al., 2024: global elevation maps gave 47–78% volume errors on small reservoirs
  • Das and Hossain, 2025: curves from SRTM elevation can be off by a few metres

Weather

Rain and evaporation, for Member C's forecast.

Rain: GPM IMERG and CHIRPSNASA (IMERG, 2000 onwards) · UC Santa Barbara (CHIRPS, 1981 onwards)

✓ Works

What it gives
Daily rain from satellites. As in the proposal, IMERG (10 km, only one day behind) is the daily input, and CHIRPS (5 km, corrected with rain gauges) is for monthly checks.
Size
IMERG: about 27 MB a day for the whole world. CHIRPS: about 3 MB a day.
Is it right for us?
  • In Sri Lanka, IMERG is better day by day and CHIRPS month by month.
  • CHIRPS version 2 ends after December 2026, so we will use version 3 (already out, same grid).
  • Final CHIRPS data runs about a month behind, with an early version about a week behind.
  • We have not yet checked either against Sri Lankan rain gauges.
How others used it
  • Perera et al., 2026: IMERG better daily, CHIRPS better monthly, in Sri Lanka
  • Funk et al., 2015 (Scientific Data): the CHIRPS paper
Ready-made tools
  • GPM IMERG (NASA)

ERA5 weatherCopernicus Climate Change Service · 25 km · 1940 onwards

✓ Checked: works for us

What it gives
Hourly weather on a 25 km grid: rain, temperature, wind and evaporation. Open-Meteo serves it free, with no key.
Size
Our daily rain for 117 reservoirs, 2020–2026: a 9 MB table.
Is it right for us?
  • In our forecast test, past rain helped a little; knowing the coming weeks' rain cut the error by another 6–9%.
  • It runs about 6 days behind.
How others used it
  • Hersbach et al., 2020: the ERA5 paper
Ready-made tools
