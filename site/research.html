Past research

How others measured water from space

What earlier studies used, how accurate they were, what goes wrong for our small tanks, and our answer. Every number here was checked against the study itself in October 2026.

In short

What the studies tell us

Heights are good

SWOT reads water height to within about 5 to 20 cm. The big reservoirs also have a gauge reading every day.

Area and shape cause the error

In India, a 17% area error became a 31% volume error. In Burkina Faso, water plants spoiled 6 of 20 reservoirs.

Nobody has done our tanks

We found no study that tracks the water in Sri Lankan tanks with SWOT. We searched in October 2026.

Member A: water maps

Has anyone mapped tank water under clouds and plants?

Yes under clouds, with radar. Not yet under water plants: that is our research question.

Clouds: mostly solved.

Radar sees through them: 85% overlap worldwide, and it worked on Sri Lankan reservoirs.

Plants: not solved by anyone yet.

That is our research question 1 (RQ1).

We will measure our own accuracy

against Kanhindawewa's drone survey and the 5 big reservoirs' daily gauges.

Member B: SWOT and storage

Has anyone turned satellite heights into stored water?

Yes for big reservoirs with records. Not yet for small tanks with no records: that is Member B's model.

Heights: solved.

SWOT is within 5–20 cm, and satellites match Sri Lankan gauges once a fixed gap is removed.

Storage with records: solved.

3–10% on big, well-recorded reservoirs.

Storage for small tanks with no records: still open.

The closest model (GRDL) is about 21% off on its curves. Ours was 6–8% of capacity on reservoirs it never saw, measured differently. This is Member B's contribution.

Member C: forecast and system

Has anyone forecast reservoir storage weeks ahead?

Yes for single reservoirs abroad, mostly days ahead. Not yet for Sri Lankan tanks, or with one model for many tanks: that is Member C's forecast.

Days ahead: solved.

For single reservoirs, forecasts one day to a week ahead are very accurate.

Weeks ahead: harder.

Errors grow with the horizon, and a model of one reservoir fails outside the range it was trained on.

Sri Lankan tanks: not done yet.

We found no published forecast for them; the department's sheet shows only today's water. One model for many reservoirs is new even in Europe.
